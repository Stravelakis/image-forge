## Start here

This page takes you from "I have never done anything like this" to "I made a folder of pictures". Go slowly. Nothing here can break your computer, and every step says what you should see, so you always know if it worked.

> **Do this first** You do not need to understand everything before you start. Read **The five ideas** (about five minutes), then follow **Step 1** onward. Come back to the rest when you want it.

**What you will have at the end:**

- Image Forge installed on your Windows computer.
- A set of pictures you asked for in plain words, in a folder you chose.
- A safe copy of your settings.
- A calm idea of what is free, what costs money, and how the app protects you.

**What you do not need:** an account, a credit card, a special computer, or any knowledge of code. You need a Windows computer and the internet.

**How long:** about 20 minutes, most of it waiting for the installer and the first pictures.

## The five ideas

Five ideas and you understand the whole app. Take them one at a time.

### 1. A picture-making machine you ask in words

Image Forge is a program on your computer. You type what you want, like *"a cute red bicycle, watercolour"*, and say how many. It makes the pictures for you. That sentence you type is called a **prompt**.

### 2. The drawing is done by someone else

Image Forge does not draw. It is like a very organised assistant who takes your order to an **artist** on the internet and collects the finished pictures. In the app these artists are called **engines**. Some of them are free, some charge a small amount per picture.

### 3. A key is a password for the artist's shop

Some artists want to know it is really you ordering, because the bill comes to you. They give you a long secret code called an **API key**. It works like a library card: whoever holds it can borrow in your name. So you keep it private. Image Forge keeps your keys on your own computer and shows them only to the artist you chose.

> **Why** One engine, called OVHcloud, needs no key at all. That is why Image Forge works the very first time you open it.

### 4. Free first, and it asks before it spends

The free artists are a little slower. The paid ones are faster or cleverer, for example at writing real words inside a picture. Image Forge will **always stop and ask** before anything costs money. It shows how many pictures, the price of each, the total, and a free option instead. Nothing is spent until you press the button that says so.

### 5. Everything is a list, and a list is a file

Behind the scenes, your pictures are a **list**: one line per picture. Image Forge shows it to you as friendly cards, and as a spreadsheet if you want. The finished pictures are normal files that you can put in a **folder** you choose. That is all there is to it.

> **In short** You type words. Image Forge takes the order to an artist (engine). The artist might ask for a password (key). Pictures come back and go into a folder. It asks before spending.

## Step 1: Download it

1. Open your web browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, any of them).
2. Go to the [downloads page](https://github.com/Stravelakis/image-forge/releases/latest). This is a page on **GitHub**, a website where programmers keep their projects. It is the official home of Image Forge.
3. Scroll to the part called **Assets**.
4. Click the file that starts with **Image.Forge.Setup** and ends with **.exe**. The numbers in the middle are the version.
5. Wait for the download. It is about 115 MB, so a coffee's worth of time on slow internet.

> **Why** The file is big because the app carries a tiny browser inside it. That is how it looks the same on every computer.

**What you should see:** the file appears in your browser's download list, usually in your **Downloads** folder.

## Step 2: Install it

1. Find the file you downloaded and double-click it.
2. Windows may show a **blue box** that says **"Windows protected your PC"**. Do not panic. Read the next box.
3. In that blue box, click **More info**. A new button appears.
4. Click **Run anyway**.
5. The installer asks where to put the app. The suggested place is fine. Click through to the end.

> **Why** Windows shows the blue box for any program from a publisher it does not know yet, because the maker has not bought a paid "this is really me" stamp (called code signing). It is not a sign that something is wrong. You only do this once. If you want to be careful, you can read the code yourself on GitHub, because the project is open source.

**What you should see:** a new **Image Forge** shortcut on your desktop and in the Start menu. The app also appears in **Settings → Apps**, where you can uninstall it later.

<details>
<summary>I do not want to install anything</summary>

Download `image-forge-portable.exe` from the same Assets list instead. Double-click it and it runs from wherever it is, like a game on a USB stick. It still remembers your settings on the computer it runs on.

</details>

## Step 3: Open it and look around

Double-click the **Image Forge** shortcut. A window opens on the **Start** screen.

At the top is a menu. You do not need most of it yet. Here is what the main parts are for:

| Menu item | What it is, in one line |
|---|---|
| **Start** | The front door. Describe what you want and press the button |
| **Made** | A shelf of everything you ever made, with the date and which artist drew it |
| **Chat** | Ask for a picture in your own words, or ask how the app works |
| **Wizards** | Step-by-step helpers for bigger jobs |
| **Gallery** | Looks (styles) and saved setups |
| **Settings** | Where artists (engines) and keys live |

> **Try it** Hold your mouse over any button. A little bubble says what it does, in plain words. You can also press the **Tab** key to move between buttons; the bubble shows then too.

## Step 4: Make your first four pictures

You are on the **Start** screen. This is the exciting part.

1. Click in the big box and type what you want. For example: *4 potion bottle icons, cute, glowing*.
2. Look at the **How many** box. It filled itself in with **4** because your sentence started with a number. You can change it.
3. Leave **Style** and **Shape** alone for now.
4. Look at the **engine** drop-down. It should say **OVHcloud**. That is the free artist who needs no key. Leave it.
5. Click the big button. It says **Make 4** (the number follows your how-many box).

**What you should see:** under the box, the pictures appear one by one. The first can take a little while. OVHcloud makes **two pictures a minute**, so four pictures take about two minutes. This is normal and it is how the free artist keeps things fair.

> **Watch out** Do not close the app while it is making pictures. Wait until all four are done. If you must stop, press **Stop** (it is on the Made screen); pictures being made are put back in the queue.

Click a picture to see it full size. Click again or press **Esc** to come back.

<details>
<summary>Nothing happened, or a picture says "failed"</summary>

Click the picture and read the red sentence. It says in plain words what went wrong. The most common cause is the free artist being busy. Wait a minute and press **Redo** on that picture. The app also retries by itself when an artist asks it to slow down.

</details>

## Step 5: Look at what you made

Click **Made** in the top menu.

At the top are numbers: how many you made, how many today, how many are waiting, how many failed, and how much money was spent (it should say nothing, because you used a free artist). Below are your pictures, each with its exact time, which artist drew it, and a status.

What you can do with a picture:

1. Click it to see it large.
2. Press **♥** if you like it, or **✕** if you do not. These are only notes for you.
3. Press **Redo** to ask the artist to draw it again. Good for ones you do not love.

Near the top there is **Redo N marked or failed**. It remakes everything you crossed out, in one go.

> **Try it** Press **GIF** on a picture you like. It turns it into a short moving picture with camera movement, free.

## Step 6: Get the pictures out of the app

Right now your pictures live inside Image Forge. To use them anywhere else, you need to put them in a **folder** on your computer. A folder is just a box on your disk that holds files.

The easiest way is **Link folder**:

1. Click **Settings**, then the **Folders** part.
2. Press **Link folder…** and choose a folder. Make a new one if you like, for example `Pictures\ImageForge`.
3. From now on, every finished picture is saved there the moment it is done.

Inside that folder, Image Forge makes tidy sub-folders: `images/` for normal pictures, `vectors/` for drawings made of lines, `lottie/` for little animations, `sheets/` for character sheets and `gifs/` for moving pictures.

Other ways to take them out are under **Download** in the top bar: a **ZIP** (many files packed into one), or only the list as a spreadsheet file, or straight into a WordPress website.

> **Why** If you do not link a folder, the app still keeps a copy of each picture in its own folder, and warns you before you close the window with pictures that have no copy anywhere. Linking your own folder is simply the tidy way.

## Step 7: Free or paid? Stay in control

You already used a free artist. Here is what the others are like.

| Artist | Cost | What you need | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| OVHcloud | free | nothing | starting straight away; slow, always square |
| Cloudflare | free | a free account id and token, no card | about 690 pictures a day |
| Pollinations | free | a free token | unlimited, slow |
| Your own computer | free | an image model installed locally | unlimited and private |
| Google (Nano Banana) | from $0.034 a picture | a key with some money on the account | real words inside pictures |
| OpenAI-style | about $0.04 a picture | a key | if you already pay for one |

**What happens when something costs money.** Before the first paid picture, a box appears. It says how many pictures will be billed, which model, the price for each, the total, and which credit pays. It also offers **free artists to switch to**. If you do nothing, nothing is spent. Only the button **Spend it** spends.

> **Do this first** If you are not sure, stay on the free artists. You can make hundreds of pictures without paying anything.

## Step 8: Add a better artist (optional)

Do this only when you want more speed or quality.

1. Click **Settings**, then **Image engines**.
2. Find the artist you want, for example **Cloudflare**. The page tells you which pieces it needs (for Cloudflare: a free **account id** and a **token**). You get them from that company's own website by making a free account there.
3. Copy the code from the company's website and paste it into the matching box in Image Forge.
4. Press **Check** next to it. This makes a real test and tells you in plain words if it worked, and if not, why.
5. If it worked, pick that artist from the drop-down on the **Start** screen.

> **Watch out** A key is a secret. Never put one in an email, a chat, a screenshot or a public file. If you ever show one by mistake, go to that company's website and make a new one; the old one then stops working.

You can add several keys for one artist. When one runs out for the day, the app switches to the next by itself.

## Step 9: Make a safe copy of your settings

This takes one minute and saves you later.

1. Go to **Settings**.
2. At the top, check the bar that says when your settings were last **saved and checked**, and how many keys are stored. (It never shows the keys themselves.)
3. Press **Back up to a file**.
4. Save the file somewhere safe and **private**.

> **Why** The backup file contains your keys, so it is as secret as the keys. If you ever lose your settings, press **Restore** and choose that file.

## Step 10: A bigger batch, with a list

When you want more than a handful, the list is your friend.

1. Open **Made**, then **Edit as a table**. This is the spreadsheet.
2. Each row is one picture. Fill the **filename** (for example `image_red_bike.png`) and the **prompt**.
3. Add as many rows as you like. You can also import a list from Excel or Google Sheets saved as **CSV**.
4. Press **Run queue**. The app makes them all.

Filenames follow simple rules (lowercase, no spaces, no characters Windows refuses). The app checks as you type and has an **auto-fix** button.

## When something goes wrong

Click the one that looks like yours.

<details>
<summary>A blue "Windows protected your PC" box</summary>

Expected, once. Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

</details>

<details>
<summary>"Image Forge could not start"</summary>

The app uses a numbered door on your computer, called a port. Another copy of Image Forge may be holding it. Close every Image Forge window (check the little icon near the clock), then open it again.

</details>

<details>
<summary>"Every key is resting"</summary>

The artist said "slow down, you have asked a lot". Image Forge rests that key and will retry by itself. You do not have to do anything.

</details>

<details>
<summary>A picture says "failed"</summary>

Click it and read the red note. Then press **Redo** again. If it keeps failing on one artist, switch to another from the drop-down.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Google says "prepayment credits are depleted"</summary>

The Google account has no money on it for pictures. Press **Check** in Settings; it explains which fix you need. Meanwhile switch to a free artist.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Settings say "NOT saved"</summary>

The app could not prove it saved. Press **Back up to a file** before closing, and keep that file.

</details>

<details>
<summary>The words in my picture are gibberish</summary>

Most artists cannot spell. Choose a Google model, or use the **Letterer**, which writes real text on top of the picture and lets you drag its corners to fit a sign.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Everything feels strange</summary>

Open **Settings → Advanced** and press **Run repair**. It untangles stuck pictures, fixes duplicate filenames and clears waiting times. Your pictures and keys are kept.

</details>

If you are still stuck, tell the makers on [GitHub](https://github.com/Stravelakis/image-forge/issues). Say what you did, what you expected and what you saw.

## A small dictionary

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Prompt** | The words you type to say what to draw |
| **Engine** | An artist on the internet (or on your computer) that draws for you |
| **API key** | A secret password for an artist's shop |
| **Free / paid** | Free artists cost nothing; paid ones charge per picture |
| **Manifest** | The list of pictures, one line each |
| **CSV** | A list saved as plain text that any spreadsheet can open |
| **Folder** | A box on your disk that holds files |
| **ZIP** | Many files packed into one |
| **Installer** | A helper that puts a program on your computer and removes it cleanly |
| **Backup** | A spare copy in case the first is lost |

## What next?

- **Look nicer:** pick a **Style** on the Start screen (there are 36).
- **Talking cartoon friend:** *Wizards → Make a puppet* draws a character in pieces so it can blink and talk in the video app, **Video Forge**.
- **Let a helper do it:** Image Forge can be driven by AI assistants such as Claude Code. That needs a bit more setup. The **English** level of this page explains it.
- **Curious how it works inside?** Switch to **Dev** in the left menu.

## Uninstall (the clean way)

Open **Settings → Apps**, find **Image Forge**, and choose **Uninstall**. It asks one question: *Remove your Image Forge data as well?* **No** keeps your settings and keys for a future reinstall. **Yes** removes them too. Your saved pictures in your own folders are never touched.
